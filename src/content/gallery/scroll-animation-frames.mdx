These are the animation frames I created for my scroll driven animation project.
Said project [has its own Projects entry](/projects/scroll-physics-animation), 
so go check that out for more details on the project, as well as a live demo 
where you can see these frames in action!

# How They Were Made
These frames were all hand-drawn by me in Procreate on my iPad.
Procreate is severely lacking in many of the animation tooling common to other
animation/drawing programs available these days, but it was still a great fit for 
this particular use case.

Drawing, positioning, and exporting all the frames took me about 3 hours, although the first hour of that was mostly just figuring out what I wanted the frames to look like.
Once I had a general idea of what I wanted, I drew the neutral frame, followed by all of the body shapes for all the other frames.
I then set to work on drawing the faces for each frame.

As a rule of thumb, I tried to keep the **eyes** and the **teeth** in the same positions relative to the bounds of the image. 
This gives the viewer a better feel for what's actually being moved around when they scroll the page. 
The **skeleton** (or, more accurately, the skull) stays in the center of the page, while the **rest** of the green guy deforms around it in response to force.
Without this, an extreme upward force frame would primarily be distinguishable from a downward one only by its **position within the viewport**, which is a much weaker visual indicator than the face's position within the shape.

I used a fixed-width brush (i.e. the width of the line doesn't change in response to speed or pressure) to draw these.
I generally gravitate towards fixed-width brushes when animating since they give a smoother, more consistent look.
It also made it easier to have other elements in the physics demo which aren't hand-drawn (like the rings the little guy flies through) match the line style for stylistic consistency and cohesion.
